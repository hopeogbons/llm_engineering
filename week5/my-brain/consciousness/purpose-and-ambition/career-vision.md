# Career Vision

## Professional Goals

### Short-Term Goals (1-2 Years)

By the end of 2025, I want RoboOffice to have 15 employees (we're at 4 now) and be generating ₦500 million in annual revenue. That means closing at least 5 more enterprise clients like the banking deal we landed last year.

I need to finish this Andela LLM Engineering program strong - top 10% of my cohort - and immediately start integrating what I've learned into RoboOffice products. I'm planning to launch an AI-powered document processing service by Q3 2025 that uses RAG and vector search.

On the technical side, I want to become proficient in production LLM deployment - not just building demos but actually shipping features that handle millions of tokens per day. I'm studying MLOps and learning how to optimize inference costs.

I'm also planning to hire my first dedicated AI/ML engineer by mid-2025. I can't do all the LLM work myself if we're going to scale this capability across our client base.

### Medium-Term Goals (3-5 Years)

By 2028, RoboOffice should be the leading automation company in Southeast Nigeria, with an office in Lagos and maybe Abuja. I want us generating ₦500 million annually with a team of 30-40 people.

I want to have raised a proper Series A round - probably $1-2 million - to fuel growth. I've bootstrapped this far, but to compete with the well-funded Lagos startups, I'll need capital. Dr. Adeyemi is already connecting me with VCs.

Personally, I want to be recognized as an expert in AI-powered automation for African markets. That means speaking at conferences (already accepted to TechCabal Battlefield 2025), writing technical articles, maybe even contributing to AI research specific to low-resource languages like Igbo.

I also want to have launched at least one open-source tool that the Nigerian dev community actually uses. Giving back to the ecosystem that's given me so much.

### Long-Term Goals (5+ Years)

In 10 years, I see RoboOffice operating across West Africa - Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya at minimum. We should have 100+ employees and be generating $10M+ in annual revenue.

I want to have successfully exited or taken RoboOffice public and used that capital to start investing in other Nigerian tech founders. Be the person for others that Dr. Adeyemi has been for me.

I envision running a venture studio that focuses on AI tools for emerging markets - the problems we solve in Nigeria (unreliable infrastructure, multiple languages, low digitization) are problems across Africa and parts of Asia.

Long-term, I'd like to teach part-time at a university - maybe UNN where my brother is studying - sharing real-world software engineering and entrepreneurship knowledge with the next generation.

### Ultimate Career Aspirations

My dream outcome is building a tech company from Nigeria that's genuinely world-class - not "good for Africa" but genuinely competitive globally. I want to prove that you don't have to be in Silicon Valley to build transformative technology.

I want to create at least 1,000 direct jobs and impact 10,000+ businesses through the tools we build. Real economic impact, not just vanity metrics.

Being recognized as one of Nigeria's leading tech entrepreneurs would be amazing - being in the same conversation as Sim Shagaya, Jason Njoku, Odunayo Eweniyi. Not for ego, but because it would mean I've actually achieved something significant.

Ultimately, I want to look back at 60 and know that I used my God-given talents fully, created opportunities for others, and left the Nigerian tech ecosystem better than I found it.

## Legacy

### Impact I Want to Make

I want Nigerian SMEs to be as automated and efficient as their Western counterparts. Right now, a pharmacy in Portsmouth uses better software than a hospital in Enugu. That gap is my target.

Every one of our automation tools should save businesses time and money, but more importantly, should free humans to do more valuable work. The accountant who stops doing manual data entry and starts doing financial analysis - that's the impact I'm after.

I want to prove that you can build a profitable, sustainable tech company in Nigeria without compromising on quality or ethics. Too many companies here cut corners. We don't.

### What I Want to Be Known For

I want to be known as the CEO who actually cared about his team - who paid fairly, invested in growth, and created a great work culture even in tough times.

Technically, I want to be known for bringing practical AI to Nigerian businesses. Not the hype, not the buzzwords, but real working systems that solve actual problems.

I want young developers from Enugu or anywhere in the Southeast to think "if he can do it from here, so can I." Regional representation matters.

Most importantly, I want to be known as someone who maintained their faith and values while building a successful business. You don't have to compromise your ethics to win in tech.

### Contributions to My Field

I'm documenting everything I'm learning about deploying LLMs in African contexts - the language challenges, the infrastructure constraints, the cost optimization strategies. I want to publish this as a comprehensive guide for other African developers.

At RoboOffice, we're building tools that handle multiple Nigerian languages and understand local business contexts. That intellectual property and knowledge should benefit the broader ecosystem.

I'm mentoring deliberately because I want to multiply myself. If I train 10 developers who go on to train 10 more, that exponential impact is worth more than anything I could build alone.

I'm also working on getting more Igbo language data into public datasets. We're creating an Igbo-English parallel corpus from our customer service data to help improve translation models. Small contribution but it matters.

### Lasting Influence

If RoboOffice outlives me and continues creating value for Nigerian businesses, that would be success.

The developers I've mentored going on to build their own companies or becoming CTO's somewhere - that's lasting influence.

When AI becomes mainstream in Africa, I want people to remember that RoboOffice was early and doing it thoughtfully, not just chasing trends.

I hope the next generation of Nigerian founders feel less alone because they can look back at stories like mine and know that it's possible to build from here.

## Career Path

### Current Position

I'm CEO of RoboOffice Ltd. - a software automation company I founded in 2019. We're based in Enugu with 8 employees and about ₦50M in annual revenue.

Day-to-day, I'm doing too many things: writing code (probably 30% of my time), managing client relationships, overseeing projects, doing strategy, handling finance. It's chaotic but I love it.

I'm also concurrently working as part of Andela's LLM Engineering program, which is technically a training/upskilling engagement but feels like a job given the intensity.

My current challenge is transitioning from founder-who-does-everything to CEO-who-leads-a-team. It's harder than it sounds.

### Desired Trajectory

Short-term (1-2 years): I need to fully transition out of day-to-day coding. Hire strong technical leaders, focus my time on strategy, fundraising, and business development.

Medium-term (3-5 years): I should be a pure CEO - setting vision, raising capital, managing key relationships, building culture. Maybe joining some advisory boards or investing in other startups on the side.

Long-term (5-10 years): Successfully exit RoboOffice or take it public, then transition to being a venture capitalist or serial entrepreneur. Running a venture studio for African tech.

Alternatively, I could see myself going into tech policy or education - using my experience to improve the Nigerian tech ecosystem at a systemic level.

### Skills to Develop

**Leadership**: I need proper management training. I'm learning on the job, but I should take a leadership course or get executive coaching.

**Finance**: I can read a P&L, but I need to get better at fundraising, cap tables, and financial strategy for growth.

**Sales**: I'm decent at selling to technical buyers, but I need to level up for enterprise sales to C-suite executives.

**Public speaking**: I'm improving, but I need to be comfortable speaking to audiences of hundreds, not just small meetups.

**Product management**: As we grow, I can't just build what clients ask for. I need to develop a product vision and manage a roadmap.

**AI/ML at depth**: The Andela program is great, but I need to go deeper into transformer architectures, fine-tuning, and deployment optimization.

### Opportunities to Pursue

**Fundraising**: I need to start actively networking with VCs, attending pitch events, and refining our fundraising story. Target: raise Series A by 2026.

**Partnerships**: Exploring partnership with bigger tech companies (Microsoft, AWS) who want to reach Nigerian SMEs.

**Speaking circuit**: More conferences, podcasts, panels. It's marketing and personal brand building.

**Advisory roles**: I've been asked to advise 2 early-stage startups. Good for network and learning.

**Writing**: Start a technical blog or newsletter. Builds authority and helps me process what I'm learning about LLMs.

**Government contracts**: Nigeria is digitizing government services - there's opportunity there if I can navigate the bureaucracy.
