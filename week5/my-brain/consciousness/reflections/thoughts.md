# Thoughts

## Introspections

### Self-Examination

Right now (late 2024), I'm questioning whether I'm spending too much time on technical work versus strategic CEO work. My natural inclination is to code, but is that where I add most value to RoboOffice anymore?

I notice I'm happiest when I'm learning something new - like these past few weeks with LLM engineering in Andela. When I'm not learning, I get restless. This suggests I need to build continuous learning into my role permanently.

I've been reflecting on my perfectionism. It's rooted in fear - fear of failure, fear of being exposed as not good enough, fear of disappointing people who believe in me. Recognizing this helps but doesn't instantly fix it.

I'm realizing my identity has been too wrapped up in RoboOffice's success. When business is good, I feel good about myself. When it's struggling, I feel like I'm failing as a person. This isn't healthy and I'm working on separating self-worth from business outcomes.

My relationship with money is complex. Grew up with financial scarcity, so part of me still operates from scarcity mindset even though RoboOffice is profitable. I notice I have irrational anxiety about money sometimes.

### Inner Dialogue

**Morning me**: "Lord, give me wisdom for today. Help me focus on what matters most." (Hopeful, dependent on God)

**Mid-work me**: "This bug makes no sense. Wait, maybe if I... yes! Got it!" (Focused, problem-solving mode, sometimes lost in flow)

**Stress me**: "Too much to do, not enough time. How will I... okay breathe. Prioritize. What actually has to happen today?" (Anxious but trying to self-regulate)

**Evening me**: "Did I actually accomplish anything today? Spent hours in meetings. Should have... no, stop. You did what you could. Tomorrow is fresh." (Reflective, sometimes self-critical, trying to be kind to myself)

**Sunday me**: "This week was wild. God is good though. He's been faithful through everything." (Grateful, grounded, peaceful)

### Questioning and Wondering

**Big questions**: Am I living my actual calling or am I just good at software? Is RoboOffice God's plan or my plan that I'm asking Him to bless? How do I know the difference?

**Business questions**: Should we raise money or stay bootstrapped? Funding accelerates growth but adds pressure and dilutes control. Which path is wiser for us?

**Career questions**: In 5 years, do I still want to be CEO? Or do I want to transition to CTO role, hire a business-focused CEO, and go back to more technical work?

**Relationship questions**: As a 29-year-old single Nigerian male, family pressure to marry is mounting. But am I ready? Do I even want marriage right now or do I just want to please people?

**Purpose questions**: Is serving Nigerian SMEs enough or should I be thinking bigger - pan-African, global impact? Am I thinking too small?

**Technical questions**: Are LLMs genuinely transformative or are they just hype cycle like so many technologies before? How should RoboOffice position relative to AI?

### Self-Awareness Observations

I notice I get energized by solving technical problems but drained by administrative tasks. I need to structure my days accordingly - hard technical thinking in the morning, admin in afternoon.

I'm realizing I'm more introverted than I thought. After full day of meetings and people, I'm exhausted. I need alone time to recharge. This is okay.

My stress shows up physically - tight shoulders, headaches, poor sleep. I'm learning to recognize these signals early and take breaks before burnout hits.

I avoid conflict more than I should. I'll let things simmer hoping they resolve themselves rather than addressing issues directly. Working on this with my therapist.

I'm competitive with myself more than with others. I don't care if other Nigerian startups raise more money; I care if I'm better than I was last quarter.

I have proud thoughts sometimes - "I'm better than most developers I meet" - and I immediately check myself. Pride is insidious.

### Cognitive Patterns

I'm a systems thinker - I see how parts interconnect. When looking at business problem, I map out dependencies, feedback loops, leverage points. This serves me well in software architecture and business strategy.

I'm also very analytical - break problems into components, analyze each, synthesize solutions. Good for debugging code and troubleshooting business issues.

But I can overthink - analysis paralysis is real for me. Sometimes I need to just decide and act, not endlessly analyze. Working on this.

I tend toward pessimism in planning (defensive pessimism) - imagine what could go wrong and plan for it. Keeps me prepared but can be draining.

Visually, I think in diagrams and architecture charts. When designing systems, I sketch boxes and arrows. Helps me think clearly.

**Productivity guilt loop**: "Spent 3 hours in meetings. Didn't write any code. Wasn't productive. But wait, those meetings were important. Leadership is productive. Or am I just justifying..." Learning to redefine productivity beyond lines of code written.

### Clear Insights

**Bootstrapping is our advantage, not limitation**: We thought we needed funding. But bootstrapping forced us to be profitable, focus on customers not investors, maintain control. Our constraint became our strength.

**Team culture is competitive moat**: Our zero turnover, team loyalty, collaborative culture - competitors can copy our product but not our culture. Invest in people.

**Nigerian market knowledge is valuable**: Large companies don't understand how Nigerian SMEs actually work. We do. This local knowledge is worth more than any technology advantage.

**Simple solutions beat complex ones**: I used to over-engineer. Client doesn't care about elegant architecture; they care about problem solved. Simplest solution that works is best solution.

**Integrity compounds**: Every time we do the right thing (honest about timeline, refund when appropriate, transparent about capabilities), trust builds. Trust leads to referrals. Integrity is long-term growth strategy.
