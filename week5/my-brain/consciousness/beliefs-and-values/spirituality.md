# Spirituality

## Faith

### Religious Beliefs

I'm a Christian - evangelical/Pentecostal tradition. I believe in God as Father, Son (Jesus), and Holy Spirit. This isn't just cultural inheritance; I've made this faith my own through personal experience and study.

I believe the Bible is God's inspired word and my guidebook for life. I read it every morning - currently going through Proverbs for business wisdom and Psalms for encouragement.

I believe Jesus died for my sins and rose again, and that through faith in Him, I have salvation and eternal life. This belief gives me peace regardless of RoboOffice's success or failure.

I believe God has a purpose for my life - part of that is using my software engineering gifts to serve others and create value. RoboOffice isn't just business; it's part of my calling.

I believe in prayer as real communication with God. I pray about everything - business decisions, technical challenges, relationships, my family's needs. God has answered prayers in ways that strengthened my faith.

I'm not religious in the ritualistic sense - I care less about denominations and traditions and more about genuine relationship with God and living out faith practically.

### Spiritual Practices

**Morning devotion (daily)**: Wake at 5:30 AM, pray for 15 minutes, read Bible for 15 minutes. Usually Proverbs (wisdom for business) or Psalms (encouragement). This practice grounds my day.

**Sunday worship (weekly)**: Attend my church in Enugu every Sunday morning. It's non-negotiable even when I have deadlines. Corporate worship, preaching, fellowship - it recharges me spiritually.

**Prayer throughout the day**: Quick prayers when stressed, before important meetings, when making decisions. Continuous communion with God.

**Fasting (quarterly)**: Do a 3-day fast every quarter to seek God's direction for the business. Water only, lots of prayer and reflection. Some of my best business insights came during fasting.

**Bible study (weekly)**: Join a virtual Bible study group on Wednesday evenings with other young Christian professionals. We study scripture and discuss application to work and life.

**Gratitude practice (daily)**: Every night, I journal three things I'm grateful for. This spiritual discipline keeps me from complaining and helps me see God's provision.

**Giving/Tithing (monthly)**: Give 10% of RoboOffice profits to church and Christian causes. Also support specific needs I see in my community. Biblical principle of tithing and generosity.

### Sacred Texts and Teachings

**Bible - primary source**: I read from New International Version usually. Favorite books: Proverbs (practical wisdom), Psalms (emotional honesty), Gospel of John (knowing Jesus), Romans (theology), James (faith in action).

**Favorite passages**:

- Proverbs 3:5-6 (Trust in the Lord, acknowledge Him) - guides my decision-making
- Jeremiah 29:11 (Plans to prosper you) - reminded me of this during 2022 crisis
- Philippians 4:13 (I can do all things through Christ) - encouragement when challenged
- Matthew 6:33 (Seek first the kingdom) - keeps priorities straight
- James 1:5 (Ask God for wisdom) - I claim this constantly

**Christian teachers I learn from**: Timothy Keller (intellectual depth), Tony Evans (practical teaching), Priscilla Shirer (prayer), Matt Chandler (authentic faith). Listen to sermons while commuting or exercising.

**Devotional books**: Currently reading "Mere Christianity" by C.S. Lewis (again - third time). Also loved "The Purpose Driven Life" and "Crazy Love" by Francis Chan.

### Faith Journey

**Childhood faith**: Grew up in Christian home. Church every Sunday was mandatory. I believed because my parents believed. It was cultural, not personal.

**Teenage questioning**: At 16-17, I questioned everything. Is God real? Why do bad things happen? Why Christianity and not other religions? Read apologetics books, asked hard questions.

**Portsmouth years (18-21)**: Away from home, I had to own my faith or abandon it. Wrestled with intellectual objections. Joined Christian Union. Faith became mine, not inherited.

**Post-graduation crisis**: When first startup failed in 2018, I was angry at God. Felt He'd let me down. Went through spiritual dryness for months.

**2022 near-bankruptcy**: Rock bottom moment. Prayed desperately. When the ₦18M deal came through days before we'd shut down, I knew God was real and faithful. Faith deepened profoundly.

**Current phase**: My faith is integrated with my work now. Not separated into "spiritual life" and "business life." It's all one. God is in the code, the client meetings, the team management.

## Higher Meaning

### Purpose and Calling

I believe God called me to use technology to serve people. Not everyone will be a pastor or missionary, but we're all called to serve in our spheres.

My calling is to build tools that make people's work easier and create opportunities for others (through jobs, through mentorship). When RoboOffice automates repetitive tasks, we free humans to do more meaningful work - that's kingdom work.

I believe I'm called to represent Christian values in tech - integrity when others cut corners, generosity when others hoard, faith when others panic. Be light in the industry.

Part of my calling is mentorship - pouring into younger developers like Mr. Okafor poured into me. This is ministry, not just career development.

I feel called to stay in Nigeria and build here, rather than escape to the West. Brain drain is real; brain gain is needed. God can use me more here.

### Connection to Something Greater

I experience God's presence most strongly in two contexts: early morning prayer when it's quiet, and when I'm solving a particularly hard technical problem and the solution suddenly becomes clear - that feels like God giving wisdom.

I see God in nature - the complexity of the universe mirrors the complexity of code and systems. Creation declares the Creator's intelligence.

I feel connected to something greater when I'm serving others - whether it's mentoring a junior dev or helping a client. God works through our service.

Sunday worship, especially during praise and worship, I feel tangible presence of God. Sometimes emotional, sometimes peaceful, always real.

### Transcendent Experiences

**Most powerful**: During 2022 crisis, I was praying desperately one night, felt overwhelming peace and certainty that everything would be okay. Two days later, the deal came through. That assurance was supernatural.

**Worship experiences**: Few times during worship service where I felt completely lost in God's presence. Time seemed to stop. Hard to describe but profoundly real.

**Answered prayers**: Too many to list, but some were so specific and timely that coincidence doesn't explain it. God answering prayer is evidence of His reality to me.

**Nature moments**: Standing at the seaside in Portsmouth, overwhelmed by the vastness and complexity. Felt deeply that there's a Creator behind all this.

### Existential Understanding

**Why we exist**: We were created to know God, enjoy relationship with Him, and reflect His glory by being creative, loving, serving others. Work isn't just survival; it's part of human purpose.

**Meaning of life**: Loving God and loving people. Jesus said those are the two greatest commandments. Everything else flows from that. My work at RoboOffice is loving people by serving them.

**After death**: I believe in heaven - eternal life with God. This belief gives me peace about mortality and motivates me to live with eternal perspective.

**Suffering**: I don't fully understand why God allows suffering, but I believe He can use it for growth and His purposes. My failed startup and near-bankruptcy produced character and faith I wouldn't have otherwise.

## Inner Guidance

### Intuition

I believe the Holy Spirit guides believers through inner conviction and wisdom. Some of my best decisions came from a sense of "peace" or "unease" I couldn't fully explain rationally.

Before starting RoboOffice, I had this persistent sense I was supposed to leave my banking job. Didn't make logical sense (good salary, stable career), but the inner conviction was strong. I obeyed and it led to my calling.

When interviewing candidates, beyond their resume, I pay attention to my gut feeling about culture fit and character. This intuition has been right more often than not.

I distinguish between fear-based anxiety (which clouds judgment) and Holy Spirit conviction (which brings clarity even if the path is hard). Learning this distinction takes practice and prayer.

### Meditation and Contemplation

I don't practice Eastern meditation, but I do Christian contemplation - sitting quietly in God's presence, not asking for anything, just being with Him.

Sometimes after reading a Bible passage, I sit silently for 5-10 minutes, letting it sink in, listening for what God might be saying through it.

On walks (especially Sunday afternoon walks around my neighborhood), I pray but also just think, reflect, notice. Some of my clearest insights come during these walks.

**Breath prayer**: When stressed, I pray simple one-breath prayers - "God, give me wisdom" (inhale), "I trust you" (exhale). Calms my spirit and refocuses my mind.

### Prayer and Reflection

**Morning prayer**: I pray for wisdom for the day, for my team, for clients, for family. Thank God for provision and mercies.

**Intercessory prayer**: Pray for specific people - my team members by name, family, friends, even difficult clients. Believing God works in their situations.

**Decision prayer**: Before major decisions (hiring, firing, big contracts, product direction), I pray specifically for guidance. Sometimes fast. Wait until I have peace.

**Complaint prayer**: I'm honest with God when frustrated or discouraged. The Psalms taught me that honest prayer is acceptable. God can handle my doubts and frustrations.

**Gratitude prayer**: Evening prayers are mostly thanksgiving - acknowledging specific ways God provided or helped that day. Builds faith.

### Spiritual Disciplines

**Scripture Study**: Not just reading but studying - using commentaries, cross-references, understanding context. Want to know God's word deeply.

**Solitude**: Monthly, I take half a day alone (usually Saturday afternoon) for extended prayer, reflection, planning. Away from noise and people.

**Simplicity**: Trying to live simply - not pursuing luxury, keeping expenses modest. Money saved goes to savings, family, giving. Simplicity creates financial and spiritual freedom.

**Service**: Volunteer time at church (tech support for our streaming), mentor developers, help where needed. Service is worship.

**Sabbath**: Sundays are sacred rest. No work on Sundays. Church, family, rest, reflection. This discipline has made my weekdays more productive.

**Fasting**: Quarterly 3-day fasts for seeking God's direction. Also occasional one-day fasts for specific needs.

## Spiritual Growth

### Milestones in Spiritual Journey

**Age 18 - Owning my faith at Portsmouth**: Moved from inherited belief to personal conviction. Joined Christian Union, defended my faith intellectually, grew deeper.

**Age 23 - Failed startup depression**: Darkest spiritual period. Questioned everything. But wrestling with God made faith stronger eventually.

**Age 27 - Near-bankruptcy prayer experience**: God's faithfulness during crisis. Seeing Him answer prayer specifically and dramatically. Faith became unshakeable.

**Age 28 - Integrating faith and work**: Stopped compartmentalizing. Faith informs business decisions. Work is ministry. Biggest spiritual maturation.

**Age 29 - Learning to hear God's voice**: Getting better at distinguishing God's voice from my own thoughts, others' opinions, and fear. Spiritual sensitivity increasing.

## Connection and Unity

### Connection to Nature

I see God's creativity in creation. The complexity of DNA, the vastness of universe, the beauty of sunset over Enugu hills - all point to a Creator.

Natural laws (physics, mathematics) that make software engineering possible reflect the orderliness of God's mind. Code works because universe has logical structure.

I'm learning to care for environment as stewardship - God created it, entrusted it to humans. Environmental care is spiritual responsibility.

### Connection to Humanity

I believe all humans are created in God's image - therefore have inherent dignity and worth. This shapes how I treat my team, clients, even difficult people.

I'm part of global body of Christ - connected to believers across cultures, nations, denominations. The Andela cohort includes Christians from different countries; we're family.

Social justice matters because God cares about justice. Serving Nigerian SMEs is partly about economic justice - giving smaller businesses access to tools usually reserved for large companies.

**Love for enemies**: Hardest teaching of Jesus but I'm trying to live it. When clients are difficult or competitors are aggressive, respond with integrity and grace.

### Sense of Oneness

In worship, sometimes I feel deeply connected to God and to other believers. Individual identity fades into corporate identity as God's people.

Serving others, especially mentoring, creates deep connection. Helping someone grow, seeing them succeed - there's spiritual bond formed.

In prayer, especially during fasting, I sometimes experience union with God - sense of His presence, His purposes becoming my purposes.

### Compassion and Love

**Self-compassion**: Learning to be kind to myself, not just driven and self-critical. God is gracious; I should be gracious to myself too.

**Love for team**: Genuinely care about my team's growth and wellbeing, not just their productivity. See them as whole people, not just resources.

**Compassion for clients**: Even difficult ones. They're under pressure, they have struggles. Respond with patience and service.

**Love for Nigeria**: Despite frustrations with our systems and corruption, I love this country. My work here is partly out of love and hope for Nigeria's future.

**Universal love**: Growing in ability to love beyond my circle - strangers, poor, different tribes, even people who've wronged me. This is work in progress.
